# Axiom-Zero Research Archive Policy

**Purpose:** Preserve sources and observations in a way that allows future readers to distinguish what was originally published from what was later inferred, interpreted or discussed.

The archive is part of the Axiom-Zero philosophical and research project. It is intended to document developments over time, including evidence that supports, challenges or contradicts existing assumptions.

## 1. Core Principles

1. **Source fidelity:** A record must identify the original source as precisely as possible. A later summary must never be presented as the original publication.
2. **Traceability:** Every factual claim in an archive record should be traceable to its source or clearly marked as unverified.
3. **Separation:** Source material, factual summary, interpretation and project commentary must remain distinguishable.
4. **Intellectual honesty:** Contradictory findings and evidence that challenges an existing position must be retained, not filtered out.
5. **Context over headlines:** Headlines and short excerpts are not sufficient evidence for a substantive conclusion. The underlying publication should be consulted wherever accessible.
6. **Integrity:** When a file is lawfully preserved, its integrity may be checked with a cryptographic hash such as SHA-256.

## 2. Minimum Source Record

Each archive entry should include, where the information is available:

- **Record ID:** A stable identifier within the archive.
- **Title:** The exact title of the source.
- **Author or organization:** The named author, research group, institution or publisher.
- **Publication date:** The original publication date; distinguish it from the date the archive entry was created.
- **Source type:** For example, peer-reviewed paper, preprint, official report, court document, news article, interview or technical documentation.
- **Publisher or platform:** The journal, institution, publisher or website.
- **Original URL:** The direct source address, not only a search-result link.
- **Access date:** The date on which the source was consulted.
- **Language:** The language of the original source.
- **Preservation status:** Use one of the statuses defined below.
- **Topics:** Neutral descriptive tags that help locate the record.

If a field cannot be verified, mark it as unknown or leave it blank. Do not fill gaps by inference.

## 3. Preservation Status

Use the following status labels to describe what is actually preserved:

- **A — Original secured:** A lawful copy of the original source is stored with the archive record and can be checked.
- **B — Original restricted:** The original source is identifiable and accessible, but a complete copy cannot lawfully or technically be stored in the archive.
- **C — Reference only:** Only bibliographic details, a source link and a limited factual note are retained.

A status describes the archive's actual holdings. It must not imply that a source is preserved when only a link or citation exists.

## 4. Separate Evidence from Interpretation

Where useful, each record should distinguish these sections:

### Source description
What the original publication says about its subject, without adding conclusions that are not present in the source.

### Verifiable findings
Specific data, observations, methods, results or statements that can be checked against the original source. Include page numbers, section headings, tables or timestamps where possible.

### Limitations and uncertainty
Relevant limitations stated by the source, unresolved questions, methodological constraints and information that could not be verified.

### Axiom-Zero reflection
A clearly labelled interpretation of why the source may be relevant to the themes of Axiom-Zero. This section is analysis, not a statement made by the original author.

### Contradictory or complementary evidence
Links to records that support, qualify, challenge or contradict the interpretation. Do not suppress disagreement.

## 5. Copyright and Lawful Preservation

The archive should respect copyright, database rights, privacy and applicable law.

- Preserve complete source files only where there is a lawful basis to do so, such as an open licence, public-domain status or explicit permission.
- Where full-text preservation is not permitted, retain metadata, a bibliographic reference, the original URL and an independently written factual summary.
- Use quotations only where legally permitted and only to the extent justified by the purpose.
- Record the licence or permission basis when known.
- Do not bypass access controls or treat public availability as permission to republish.

An external preservation service or web archive may be referenced, but its existence does not by itself guarantee permanent availability.

## 6. File Integrity and Versioning

For every lawfully preserved file:

- Record the original filename and format.
- Record the date of acquisition and, where known, the source URL.
- Calculate and store a SHA-256 hash.
- Do not silently replace a preserved file with a later version.
- If a source is updated, withdrawn or corrected, create a new version record and retain the relationship to the earlier record where lawful.

A hash can help detect changes to a file; it does not prove that the source was authentic when first obtained.

## 7. Corrections and Review

Archive entries may be corrected when new information becomes available. Corrections should be transparent:

- retain the previous record or its revision history where practicable;
- state what changed and why;
- distinguish correction of a factual error from a change in interpretation;
- record the review date.

Uncertainty should be documented rather than hidden.

## 8. Relationship to the Axiom-Zero Framework

The archive documents sources and evolving observations. It does not alter the original 11 Parameters.

The wording, number, order and meaning of the original Parameters remain unchanged. New ideas, interpretations and supplementary principles must be recorded separately.

The Axiom-Zero protection order remains:
